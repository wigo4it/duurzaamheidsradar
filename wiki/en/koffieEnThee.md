# Coffee and Tea

## What does this mean?
he Dutch drink an average of 2 to 4 cups of coffee per day. According to BBC calculations, the ecological footprint of a cup of coffee is about ten times greater than that of a cup of tea. If you drink one cup of coffee every day for a year, the emissions are equivalent to driving 640 kilometers in an average petrol car.
The average coffee from Douwe Egberts or tea from Pickwick is harvested and processed by millions of workers under poor living conditions and low wages. Unfair trade practices mean that farmers do not always receive a fair price for their products. In addition, production has a significant environmental impact, such as deforestation, high water consumption, and pollution from pesticides.

By choosing products with the following certifications, you contribute to better working conditions and more environmentally friendly practices:

- Rainforest Alliance
- Fairtrade
- UTZ Certified

## How are we doing?
We have researched our coffee and tea. The coffee is from the brand Capriole and only carries the Fairtrade certification. The tea is from Rioba and also has the Fairtrade certification. We cannot simply change our coffee supplier, as it is tied to the contract for our coffee machines. This is a good example of how, at the time, we did not take sustainability into account when selecting a supplier.

We aim in the future to purchase more sustainable coffee that does carry the Rainforest Alliance certification and is roasted in the Netherlands under better environmental conditions, such as <a href="https://hesselinkkoffie.nl/over-ons/duurzaamheid/">Hesselink Koffie</a>. In addition, we could also consider short-chain alternatives such as <a href="https://wilder-land.com/">Wilderland</a>. In short:

- We are doing reasonably well with the Fairtrade certification.

- We can improve by seeking suppliers who also carry the Rainforest Alliance certification.

- We have learned that we must take sustainability into account in contracts from now on.

- We can improve by thinking more in terms of short supply chains.
