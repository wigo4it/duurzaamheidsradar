# Right Size Provisioning

## What does this mean?
Right size provisioning is a way to align IT infrastructure precisely with the amount of allocated resources (such as CPU, memory, storage, and network capacity) to the actual needs of an application or workload. The goal is to avoid allocating too many resources (overprovisioning) or too few (underprovisioning), thereby saving costs and increasing efficiency.

- Overprovisioning means running more hardware or virtual machines than strictly necessary, leading to unnecessary energy consumption. By only using the required capacity, you reduce the energy consumption of servers—whether on-premises or in the cloud. This in turn lowers the CO₂ emissions generated by data centers.

- Underprovisioning can negatively impact the performance of your applications, as they may not have enough computing power or memory to function optimally. Right size provisioning ensures that applications receive the right amount of resources to consistently perform well without unnecessary overhead.

- In cloud environments, the ability to scale quickly based on changing needs is crucial. Right size provisioning ensures flexibility by allowing resources to be adjusted at any moment without waste. This promotes efficiency and ensures that IT infrastructure remains scalable without unnecessary costs or capacity issues.

- Cloud providers such as AWS, Azure, and Google Cloud offer capabilities to automatically scale
 and adjust resources based on actual demand. Applying right size provisioning in cloud environments means fully leveraging the scaling advantages of the cloud while minimizing energy and resource waste. This enables more sustainable choices and improves energy efficiency.

## How are we doing?
At Wigo4it, several actions related to right size provisioning were carried out during ['CleanupFest'](wiki.html?page=cleanupFest):

- Azure Advisor indicated that several VMs were oversized; downscaling SKUs is the solution and an example of right size provisioning.

- Configuring Agent Pools (ScaleSet agents) so that only a few idle ones are running.

- Scaling down SQL managed instances from 8 cores to 4 cores (saving about €800 per month).

- Configuring images with only the tools and software actually needed.
