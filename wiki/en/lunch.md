# Lunch

## What does this mean?
The impact of lunches in the business world is significant, both in terms of food waste and the environmental impact of the different types of food offered. In corporate restaurants and canteens, a large part of the food supply is wasted. Studies show that 10–20% of food is often thrown away unused. This is due to overproduction, poor planning, and fluctuating numbers of diners.

Meat dishes are traditionally the most common in business meals and have by far the highest environmental impact, with significant greenhouse gas emissions, land use, and water consumption.

Fish is less common than meat, but still an important part of business meals due to the perception of fish as a healthier option. The environmental impact of fish varies depending on the fishing method and species, with overfishing and unsustainable fishing practices being major concerns.

The demand for vegetarian and vegan options is steadily growing, driven by health-conscious employees and increasing environmental awareness. Plant-based diets generally have a lower environmental impact than animal-based products, with fewer greenhouse gas emissions, lower water use, and less land use. Vegan diets are typically the most sustainable, with the lowest greenhouse gas emissions, minimal water consumption, and least land use. Promoting vegetarian and vegan options in business meals can therefore significantly reduce the environmental impact.

## How are we doing?
Most colleagues get something to eat near the office or bring their own food. We do not have a company cafeteria. Occasionally, lunches are organized, for example during our sacred quarterly meetings or when customers/suppliers visit with a large group around lunchtime. What do we already do, and what not yet?

We are not yet doing enough to encourage healthy eating. There is a lot of unhealthy food available in and around our office.

On busy days, we do provide fresh fruit: cherry tomatoes, pears, apples, bananas, kiwis, dates, etc.

When we order lunch, it is still 50/50. Half is meat and fish, the other half vegetarian and vegan.

We hardly throw anything away. When we order lunch, there are always colleagues who, even at 4:30 p.m., think: “Let me just eat this soggy sandwich.”
