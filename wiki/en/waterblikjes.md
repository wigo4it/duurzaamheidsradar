# Water Bottles

## What does this mean?
For us, water comes from the tap, not from a plastic bottle. The costs of bottled water are astounding when you consider how much you actually pay for production and distribution compared to the water itself. It's even more shocking when you consider the negative environmental effects at every stage of its existence. Every time you buy a plastic bottle, you pay 90% for the costs associated with producing that plastic. We deliberately put only one bullet point here so the message comes across more clearly:

- It takes **three** times the amount of water to produce and fill one water bottle.
Additionally, the production process also generates greenhouse gases in the form of CO2. Besides the costs of energy and raw materials, the transport of bottled water also contributes to the ecological footprint. All these factors contribute to the high economic costs of plastic water bottles.

## How are we doing?
At Wigo4it we no longer have plastic water bottles, but have switched to cans. To be completely honest, that's also nonsense. That's why we intend to stop buying cans of sparkling and regular water. In the past we had a kettle that was turned on dozens of times a day, which is also not sustainable. That's why we recently purchased a Quooker. If you need more than 5 liters of boiling water per day, a Quooker is more sustainable than turning on the kettle dozens of times. Our Quooker provides both boiling and sparkling water. In short:

- We really must stop buying cans of (sparkling) water.
- Bottles of water are completely out of the question.
- Use the tap or Quooker for all water.
- A Quooker saves water, you only use what you need.
