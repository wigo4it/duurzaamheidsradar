# Reduce Idle Time

## What does this mean?
Reducing idle time is an important sustainability principle, as it focuses on minimizing energy consumption and resource waste when systems are not actively being used. By ensuring that resources—such as virtual machines or AKS nodes—do not run unnecessarily during periods of inactivity, a significant amount of energy can be saved and CO₂ emissions reduced. This is especially relevant in cloud environments, where resources can be dynamically scaled based on usage. Why else is it wise to reduce idle time as much as possible?

- Shutting down inactive processes ensures that resources are only used when they are truly needed. This leads to more efficient use of hardware and software, meaning less wasted CPU time, memory, and storage.

- Reducing idle time means lower energy consumption, which in turn reduces electricity demand—and in many cases, that electricity is still generated by fossil fuels.

- Some resources offer a built-in function called auto-shutdown. This ensures that resources are automatically turned off outside working hours or when inactive, preventing unnecessary waste.

## How are we doing?
Wigo4it is doing quite well when it comes to avoiding idle time:

- We use Azure Kubernetes Autoscaling, which automatically shuts down inactive pods. We also use Virtual Machine ScaleSets for our pipelines, with only 1 or 2 standing by.

- We are exploring whether we can put unavoidable idle time at night to good use. (This still needs further elaboration.)

- We still have a small amount of legacy running. This often sits idle at night. By converting this legacy into more cloud-native solutions, we can make further progress.
